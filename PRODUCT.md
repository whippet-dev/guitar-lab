# Guitar Lab

Guitar Lab is a personal hobby project for learning guitar and music theory on an iPhone and a Windows laptop. A public GitHub Pages copy makes it easy to try. Prioritise useful features, a polished mobile experience, accurate theory and quick iteration with straightforward code.

The app connects sounds, note names and fretboard shapes, then puts them to work in riffs, chords and improvisation. The starting point is someone who can play songs and chords with limited theory knowledge. Introduce ideas and terminology as they become useful, with room for beginners and deeper study.

## The learning experience

- Make it easy to start a personalised, roughly five-minute practice session and to pause and resume it. Give each session a clear focus, revisit ideas that need practice and introduce new concepts gradually. Lessons and the explorer are always available independently.
- Teach in friendly, conversational British English. Use short explanations, show the idea on the fretboard and let the learner hear or try it. Explain unfamiliar terms when they first matter.
- Give specific feedback that helps the learner decide what to try next. Offer hints, worked answers and optional depth such as **Why does this work?**
- Design around an iPhone touch screen and keep the Windows laptop layout responsive. Pair listening and on-screen tasks with optional ways to apply the idea on acoustic or electric guitar. Preserve offline use where practical and keep installation and updates easy.
- Use readable text, clear focus, meaningful labels and comfortable touch targets. Keep accessibility work focused on making the app usable in everyday practice.
- Keep progress descriptive, private to the device and useful for choosing what to revisit. Keep practice free of gamification, including XP, coins, rankings, artificial rewards, lives and streak pressure.

## Current learning areas

Learn the neck, compare intervals, build scales and chords, follow a progression, match sounds and place a phrase in time. Daily practice connects these ideas. The explorer and individual lessons are also available on their own, with audio, local progress and offline use. See the [curriculum](CURRICULUM.md).

## Working on the project

- Review the existing code and guidance before significant changes. Make focused changes that are useful on their own and preserve the working learning flows.
- Make routine decisions autonomously. Ask when a decision materially affects functionality, cost or direction. Keep project documentation short and update the existing files when a decision changes.
- Prioritise getting working changes onto GitHub Pages for direct testing on iPhone. At the end of a change, say what changed, what can be tested and what still needs attention. Call out any action the owner needs to take.
