# Curriculum

| Sequence | What learners investigate | Application | Release |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Landmarks | Standard tuning, open strings and the same note names at fret 12 | Find reference points without counting from scratch | Initial |
| Musical alphabet | Semitones, sharps, flats and natural notes, including E–F and B–C | Work out a note from a nearby landmark | Initial introduction |
| Octaves | 12 semitones on one string and repeating note names | Move a short riff to another register | Initial introduction |
| Intervals | Distances, qualities and the relationship to a root | Hear and recreate the movement in a riff | Explorer preview |
| Scales | Major, natural minor, scale degrees and parallel keys | Hear how scale degrees pull towards the root | Explorer preview |
| Pentatonics | Major and minor pentatonic, position connections and root locations | Move a phrase between positions | Explorer preview |
| Chords | Triads, inversions, chord tones and common progressions | Connect open chords with chord tones higher up the neck | Planned |
| Keys | Diatonic chords, tonic and scales that fit a progression | Target chord tones over a progression | Planned |
| CAGED | Five movable shapes, roots and overlapping positions | Connect rhythm and lead playing in a familiar key | Planned |
| Rhythm and listening | Pulse, subdivisions, interval recognition and call and response | Play with intention and hear the sound before reaching for it | Planned throughout |
| Practical lead | Motifs, phrasing, space and resolving notes | Improvise over simple changes | Planned |

## Exercise formats

- **Interactive demonstration.** Follow a short explanation, select highlighted positions and hear the pitches. An optional detail explains the relationship.
- **Find a note.** Choose a fret location in the requested area. Exercises can check the note name, an exact pitch or a specific position. Feedback uses the selected string and fret and explains the answer.
- **Fretboard practice.** In the explorer, find a named note or name a marked position. Keep going at your own pace, with questions drawn from the frets in view.
- **Practice sequence.** Start with a short demonstration, work through three connected prompts, then take one idea into playing. One prompt revisits a prerequisite and the remaining prompts apply the idea in related positions.
- **Planned formats.** Match sounds, tap rhythm, build a chord, play a response and reflect on an improvisation task. Each can define prerequisites and feedback independently of its visual input.

Daily practice begins with the first unestablished topic. Clear first responses introduce the next topic. Misses and worked answers suggest revisiting the same idea. Once all available topics are established, elapsed time and previous difficulty guide review. Learners can open any available topic on their own.
