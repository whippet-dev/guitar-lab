# Curriculum

| Sequence | What learners investigate | Application | Release |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Landmarks | Standard tuning, open strings and the same note names at fret 12 | Find reference points without counting from scratch | Initial |
| Musical alphabet | Semitones, sharps, flats and natural notes, including E–F and B–C | Work out a note from a nearby landmark | Initial introduction |
| Octaves | 12 semitones on one string and repeating note names | Move a short riff to another register | Initial introduction |
| Intervals | Compare thirds, fifths and octaves on a movable starting note | Hear the distance and find it across strings | Guided lesson and practice |
| Scales | Build major and natural minor with W/H steps and degree formulas | Compare parallel scales and hear the change in the third | Guided lesson and practice |
| Pentatonics | Connect five positions, compare relative major and minor, and reveal tonic triads | Cross into a neighbouring position and find alternate locations for a note | Guided lesson and practice |
| Chords | Build major, minor, diminished and augmented triads and compare inversions by ear | Hear how changing a chord tone or the bass changes the sound | Guided lesson and practice |
| Keys and changes | Compare chords made from one major scale, using key and progression controls | Test the same melody note against changing chords | Guided lesson and practice |
| CAGED | Compare five movable forms with their open shapes and find shared frets | Find the same chord up the neck | Guided lesson and practice |
| Voicings | Compare triad inversions on adjacent strings | Play the same chord with a different bass or top note | Guided lesson and practice |
| Listening | Match a heard note to the fretboard and compare a guess to the target | Hum and find small intervals by ear | Guided lesson and practice |
| Rhythm | Place notes on beats and offbeats in a playable one-bar pattern | Leave space in a phrase while keeping the same pulse | Guided lesson and practice |
| Practical lead | Motifs, phrasing, space and resolving notes | Improvise over simple changes | Planned |

## Exercise formats

- **Interactive demonstration.** Follow a short explanation, select highlighted positions and hear the pitches. An optional detail explains the relationship.
- **Find a note.** Choose a fret location in the requested area. Exercises can check the note name, an exact pitch or a specific position. Feedback uses the selected string and fret and explains the answer.
- **Build a chord.** Choose the missing chord tones on the fretboard, hear them together and check the result. Alternate positions and octaves of the same notes are accepted.
- **Listen and match.** Hear a pair of notes, then find the second pitch. Compare an attempt to the target or use a visual hint.
- **Place the rhythm.** Choose which quaver subdivisions have a note onset in a 4/4 bar. Hear the result against a click or follow the visual beat, then check the placement.
- **Fretboard practice.** In the explorer, find a named note or name a marked position. Keep going at your own pace, with questions drawn from the frets in view.
- **Practice sequence.** Start with a short demonstration, work through three connected prompts, then take one idea into playing. One prompt revisits a prerequisite and the remaining prompts apply the idea in related positions.
- **Planned formats.** Match sounds, tap rhythm, play a response and reflect on an improvisation task.

Daily practice builds on two distinct correct first attempts per topic. It mixes the focus material with a prerequisite or an overdue review, prefers questions absent from the most recent session and uses elapsed time and past difficulties to choose reviews. Learners can open any lesson on its own.
